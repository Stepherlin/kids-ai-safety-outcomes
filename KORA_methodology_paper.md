# KORA HERLIN: A Systematic Review Framework for Mapping GenAI Developmental Outcomes in Children

**Stéphie Herlin — May 2026**

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This paper describes the methodology behind KORA HERLIN, a structured evidence review of generative AI effects on child development. The framework asks one core question: what does peer-reviewed and grey literature tell us about how GenAI tools affect children across eight developmental domains? The review covers children and adolescents aged 3 to 18. It draws on studies published between 2015 and 2025, across all geographies and languages where English abstracts exist.

---

## 2. Corpus Construction

We built the corpus across five databases: PubMed, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, arXiv, and ERIC. Search strings combined GenAI exposure terms (large language model, chatbot, AI tutor, generative AI) with developmental outcome terms mapped to each of the eight spheres. We ran targeted queries per sphere to correct for coverage gaps in underrepresented domains such as sensorimotor and metacognitive development.

The initial pool contained 1,802 records. We applied a two-stage screening process. Stage one removed duplicates and studies with no measurable developmental outcome. Stage two excluded records where the AI exposure was not identifiable as generative. The final corpus contains 1,227 included studies. We also integrated grey literature from 14 AI labs and EdTech builders, adding 87 records flagged as institutional reports rather than peer-reviewed articles.

---

## 3. Evidence Weighting

We adapted the Przybylski and Weinstein (2017) methodological quality framework to weight each study. The framework assigns a tier score based on four criteria: pre-registration, sample size above 100 participants, reported effect size, and replication or independent validation.

Strict-tier studies score 5 points. They meet all four criteria. Moderate-tier studies score 3 points. They are peer-reviewed with sample size above 30 and a reported effect size. Loose-tier studies score 0.1 points. They are included but do not meet the above thresholds.

These scores feed into sphere-level aggregations. They do not exclude studies. All 1,227 records appear in the corpus. The tier affects how much weight a study carries in the summary rate calculations.

---

## 4. Developmental Taxonomy

KORA organises child development into eight primary spheres and one contextual layer. The eight spheres are: academic and learning, neurocognitive, affective and emotional, psychosocial, creative and expressive, environmental context, sensorimotor, and metacognitive. Each sphere maps to established developmental psychology frameworks, drawing on Bronfenbrenner's ecological model and domain-specific developmental theories.

Each sphere has a set of 22 underlying causal mechanisms. These mechanisms describe the pathways through which GenAI exposure produces developmental effects. They fall into four groups: cognitive (7), affective (5), social (5), and developmental (5). The mechanism layer allows researchers to trace not just what effects appear, but through what process.

The contextual layer captures environmental conditions that moderate outcomes. It draws on the Przybylski digital context model and covers four sub-contexts: digital environment, physical environment, social environment, and institutional context.

---

## 5. Directional Coding

Each included study receives an effect direction code. The four codes are: positive (beneficial effect on the developmental outcome), negative (harmful or null-beneficial effect), null (no significant effect found), and mixed (conflicting results within the same study).

Two independent coders assigned direction codes to a random sample of 200 studies. Agreement rate was 87%. Disagreements went to a third coder for adjudication. The remaining 1,027 studies received codes from the primary reviewer, with a 10% spot-check by the second coder.

---

## 6. Industry Bias Analysis

We identified industry-affiliated studies by checking author institution fields against a list of 11 organisations: OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Meta, Microsoft, Apple, Anthropic, Khan Academy, Duolingo, and three EdTech publishers. A study is flagged as industry-affiliated if at least one author works at a listed organisation.

Of the 1,227 included studies, 143 are industry-affiliated. We computed positive outcome rates separately for independent and industry-affiliated studies within each sphere. The gap between these two rates is the industry bias indicator. The pattern shows domain-specific bias rather than a uniform positive skew. Academic and neurocognitive spheres show industry-affiliated studies reporting positive outcomes at rates 23 to 45 percentage points above independent studies. Affective and psychosocial spheres show the reverse: industry-affiliated studies report positive outcomes at rates 7 to 14 percentage points below independent studies. This asymmetry suggests strategic publication patterns rather than neutral research output.

---

## 7. Limits and Next Steps

Three gaps remain in the current corpus. Non-English-language studies are underrepresented: only 6% of included studies originate from non-Anglophone research traditions. Age granularity is coarse: many studies report findings for broad age bands (e.g., 6–18) without age-stratified results. The sensorimotor and metacognitive spheres have thin coverage, with 48 and 18 studies respectively.

Version 10 of the corpus will address the first gap by running translated search strings in Mandarin, French, and Spanish databases. Age-stratified analysis and expanded sensorimotor queries are planned for Q3 2026.
