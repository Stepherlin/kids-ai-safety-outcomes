Industry Bias Analysis

Comparing positive outcome rates between independent researchers and industry-funded or industry-affiliated studies across all 8 KORA developmental spheres.

What counts as "industry"

A study is classified as industry-affiliated if ≥1 co-author is employed at an AI lab (Google, OpenAI, Microsoft, Meta, Anthropic, etc.) or an edtech builder (Duolingo, Khan Academy, MagicSchool AI, Carnegie Learning, etc.), or if the study discloses industry funding. Classification is based on author affiliations, acknowledgements, and product mentions as reported in the study text.

Key findings
Academic — gap +27pp · n=64
Neurocognitive — gap +45pp · n=7 ⚠
Creative — gap +23pp · n=8
Environmental — gap −1pp · n=7
Affective — gap −7pp · n=12
Psychosocial — gap −14pp · n=10
Interpretation

Academic, neurocognitive, and creative spheres show a strong industry positivity bias: industry-funded studies are 1.5–2× more likely to report positive outcomes. This aligns with publication bias and the commercial incentive to demonstrate learning gains.

Affective and psychosocial spheres show the opposite pattern: industry studies are slightly more likely to report negative or null outcomes. This likely reflects that companies conducting internal safety and wellbeing evaluations find harms they don't publish widely — and independent researchers focusing on these domains are motivated by concern rather than product validation.

Small sample caveat: Industry sample sizes are 7–64 per sphere. Neurocognitive (+45pp) is based on only 7 industry studies and should be interpreted cautiously.

Slope chart guide

Each line connects the independent positive rate (left) to the industry positive rate (right) for one sphere. Lines sloping upward = industry more positive. Lines sloping downward = industry less positive. Line thickness ∝ √(n industry studies). Faded circles = no industry studies identified.
